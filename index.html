How I work

The way I run every project, whatever the platform or the client.

  1. 01

    Agree the scope in writing

    Before anything is built, we agree a short written scope. It says who the learners are, what they must be able to do afterwards, what the first release includes and what waits for later. It also names who signs off each stage, and how we'll know it worked. One page is usually enough. It keeps everyone honest when new ideas turn up halfway through, and it means you know exactly what you're paying for. On the ALW platform, a one-page scope sheet kept a multi-country build on track.

  2. 02

    Start from what you have

    I start with your platform, your content and your tools, and with the people who already know the subject. Your experts bring the depth; I shape it into learning that works. Where something proven already does the job, I adapt it before anyone writes new code. That's quicker, cheaper and easier for your team to run after I've gone. On SITCIN, I kept the existing Drupal platform, fixed what was broken and built on it instead of starting again.

  3. 03

    Test with real users early

    A clickable prototype in the real authoring tool or platform goes to a few of your learners before the full build. On SITCIN, the three-country pilot found silent data loss that no review had caught, and I fixed it before launch.

  4. 04

    Hand it over documented

    A project is finished when your team can run it without me. You get the source files, an admin guide and a walkthrough with the people who will maintain it. etir.org has been in UNECE's hands since 2021, with the 30-page admin guide I wrote for the secretariat.

Project stages

01Discovery and scope

I start with the problem, the people and the systems already in place. I talk to subject matter experts, managers and some of the learners, look at the current platform and its data, and write down what the first release will include.

The result is a short scope the client signs. Every later change is checked against it.

02Learning and platform design

Learning design and technical design run together. I map the learner journey, the content structure and the integrations (LMS, SCORM, xAPI, sign-in) at the same time, so a design never depends on a feature the platform can't deliver.

I prototype early in the real authoring tool or platform, so reviewers react to something they can click.

03Build and pilot

I build in Storyline, Rise, Open edX, Moodle or Drupal, depending on what the client already runs. I use AI tools such as Synthesia and text-to-speech where they save time and budget.

A pilot with real users comes before the full launch. Localisation covers navigation, messages and prompts as well as the script.

04Launch and handover

Launch includes the communications, testing, deploying and publishing the SCORM on the LMS, and the first round of fixes. Handover means an admin guide, a clean repository and a client team that can run the platform.
