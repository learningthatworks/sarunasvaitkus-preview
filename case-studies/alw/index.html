Advanced Learning Company, Riyadh

A mentor-led learning platform for Saudi K‑12 students, live in three and a half months

Saudi schools closed in spring 2020 and Advanced Learning Company needed its platform running by September. I was its learning technology lead and solution architect, the client's technical voice with the build partner EPAM.

The situation

ALC had planned a digital learning service for Saudi school students for years. When COVID-19 closed the schools in spring 2020, the plan became urgent. Parents needed something their children could use at home, built on the national curriculum, in Arabic and English, with real teachers involved.

I had worked with ALC since it was new. In 2015 and 2016 I worked on site as its eLearning solution specialist: the project plan, the technology chapters, the RFPs and supplier selection with the CEO. A first build started and stopped when funding ran out. From 2018 I advised the CEO on how to restart. When funding came back in March 2020, I returned to lead the technology side.

What made it hard

The budget and the launch date were fixed. ALC had no learning technology team of its own, so EPAM needed one person who could speak for the product, the content and the platform at once. Lessons and platform had to be ready together, and the whole interface had to work in two languages with different reading directions. Everyone worked remotely, across five countries, with daily workshops between Riyadh, London, Moscow and Minsk.

Decisions

  1. Mar 2019

    Adapt a proven platform and pilot with real schools

    The first build had lost time in 2016 by starting from scratch with no real users. I recommended a learning experience platform on existing technology: student experience first, learning data tracked from day one, and one shared content library.

  2. Apr 2020

    Make one scope sheet the master document

    EPAM's draft statement of work described scope in general terms. I proposed that the detailed scope spreadsheet become the reference for every change request and defect. The CEO agreed, and most arguments about scope were settled before they started.

  3. Apr 2020

    Ship a smaller first release

    We took tutors and parent changes to the learning path out of the first release, and kept text-message login and smart TV support out.

  4. Jun 2020

    Ask for the workflows behind the screens

    Design screens were arriving without the workflows behind them. I raised it, which led to a scope meeting with EPAM leadership and a joint plan of user stories by sprint.

  5. Aug 2020

    Upload only approved lessons

    I agreed a go-live rule: test uploads for teacher review first, then one clean bulk upload of approved lessons only.

What shipped

  • Student, parent, mentor and admin accounts, with parent invitations
  • Learning paths, subject pages and a weekly lesson plan
  • Live classes and one-to-one mentor sessions through Zoom
  • A SCORM lesson player, video playback and authoring inside the platform
  • Dashboards, badges for units completed on time, and notifications
  • Subscriptions, coupons and online payment
  • A full Arabic and English interface

My part

  • The content structure: grade, subject, unit, topic and lesson, with each lesson in five short parts from warm-up to reinforcement
  • A shared glossary and a working wireframe of the student and parent areas during discovery
  • A model lesson in Storyline 360, and how lessons report progress to the platform
  • The brief for the content vendor and the first quality check of its lessons
  • The curriculum master sheet used to load every subject, unit and lesson
  • The translation workflow for system text
  • The company website, the product landing page and analytics

Commercial sales started on 30 September 2020 through a partner channel. ALC dates the public launch to 2021, under new leadership. The platform has been redesigned since then, with additions such as an AI tutor, so today it looks and works differently from the 2020 version. At the end of my contract the CEO thanked me in writing for the platform, the educational content, the website and the sample lessons.

What I would do again, or differently

  • Agree how the product will be sold before designing it. It was designed for parents buying directly; when sales moved to partners, much of it had to change.
  • Parents pay, but students decide whether the platform gets used. Engagement features belong in the first release, even in a simple form.
  • Content is half the product. Lesson standards, naming, tracking and upload rules have to be ready before the platform is, or launch waits for content.
