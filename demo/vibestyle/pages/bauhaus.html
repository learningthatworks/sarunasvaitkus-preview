Bau
haus

A German art school that lasted only 14 years and still shapes almost every website you use. Basic shapes, primary colours and honest function come first.

Open the kit Copy prompt

What defines it

Bauhaus design reduces everything to basic geometry and primary colour. Form follows function, and decoration only stays if it helps.

Basic shapes

Circle, square and triangle are the whole alphabet of form.

Primary colours

Red, yellow and blue with black and white. Nothing mixed or muted.

Geometric type

Sans-serifs built from circles and straight lines, often set in bold blocks.

Form follows function

Every element has a job. The grid and structure stay visible and honest.

Where it came from

The Bauhaus school moved three times in 14 years, and its teachers and students spread its ideas around the world.

  1. 1919

    A new school

    Architect Walter Gropius founds the Bauhaus in Weimar, combining art, craft and industry.

  2. 1923

    Shapes and colours

    Wassily Kandinsky surveys students about which colour fits which shape. Many answer yellow triangle, red square and blue circle.

  3. 1925

    Dessau and type

    The school moves to Dessau, and Herbert Bayer designs a lowercase-only universal alphabet.

  4. 1933

    Forced to close

    Under Nazi pressure the school shuts down in Berlin, and many teachers emigrate.

  5. 1937

    New Bauhaus

    László Moholy-Nagy opens the New Bauhaus in Chicago, spreading the approach through America.

Motion study

Bauhaus motion is mechanical and exact. Shapes rotate in quarter turns and return to their place.

Kandinsky's pairing: yellow triangle, red square, blue circle. The kit uses it for badges and highlights.

Prompt it

A ready prompt for AI design and coding tools. It names exact tokens and bans the usual defaults, so the output keeps this style's point of view.

Design a responsive landing page for a design and architecture museum in an authentic Bauhaus style.

Palette: Paper #EFEDE8, Red #D7263D, Yellow #F5C518, Blue #1B4DB1, Black #141414, White #FFFFFF.
Typography: League Spartan 800 for blocky headlines, Figtree for body. Uppercase tracked buttons.
Layout: The hero is a thick-lined block grid: a big title block, a yellow block with a blue circle, a red square, a black block with a yellow triangle, a blue block and a white text block with buttons.
Signature details: Only red #D7263D, yellow #F5C518, blue #1B4DB1, black and white. 3px black lines, no radius on blocks, pure circles and triangles placed on the grid.
Components: Rectangular red and blue buttons that turn yellow on hover, cards with thick borders, round badge "dots", tricolour progress bars.
Motion: Quarter-turn rotations with mechanical easing, shapes returning to place.
Avoid: gradients, pastels, rounded cards, shadows, decorative illustration, yellow text on white.
Do not fall back on generic AI defaults: no centered hero with a gradient blob, no identical rounded cards with soft grey shadows, no purple-to-blue gradients, no emoji icons, unless this style explicitly calls for them.
Accessibility: body text contrast of at least 4.5:1, visible keyboard focus on every control, labels on all form fields, and respect prefers-reduced-motion.
Deliver one self-contained HTML file with CSS variables for every color, font, radius and shadow token.

Common mistakes

Where this style usually goes wrong, and how to keep it honest.

Primary overload

Red, yellow and blue everywhere feels like a toy box. Let white space and black carry the page.

Yellow text

Yellow on white fails contrast. Use yellow only as a shape or background.

Shapes as clip art

Random scattered shapes lose meaning. Place them on the grid with purpose.

Fake Bauhaus fonts

Many "Bauhaus" display fonts are hard to read. Keep body text in a clean geometric sans.
