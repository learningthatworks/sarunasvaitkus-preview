Flat Design

The great clean-up of 2013. Textures, gloss and fake leather were thrown out, and interfaces turned into solid colours, simple icons and long diagonal shadows.

Open the kit Copy prompt
✉☁♫☀✎♥

What defines it

Flat design removed the imitation of real materials from interfaces. Everything became simple shapes and solid colour, focused on clarity and speed.

Solid colours

Bright, flat fills from a set palette, with no gradients or textures.

Long shadows

A signature trick: a diagonal shadow stretches far from icons and letters.

Simple icons

Pictograms reduced to basic outlines and shapes.

Clean typography

Friendly sans-serifs at clear sizes replace embossed, textured labels.

Where it came from

Flat design was a reaction against years of glossy, textured interfaces.

  1. 2006

    A music player

    An early portable media player interface uses big type and flat colour, pointing the way.

  2. 2010

    Tiles and type

    A mobile operating system launches a tile-based interface built on typography and flat colour.

  3. 2012

    Desktops go flat

    Desktop operating systems adopt full-screen flat tiles.

  4. 2013

    iOS 7

    Apple removes textures and gloss from its mobile interface, and flat becomes the industry default.

  5. 2014

    Adding depth back

    Material Design brings back subtle shadows and layers, often called flat 2.0.

Motion study

Flat motion is clean and mechanical. Tiles flip to reveal content, like the live tiles of early flat phones.

Each tile rotates on its X axis with a small delay, creating a wave of flips.

Prompt it

A ready prompt for AI design and coding tools. It names exact tokens and bans the usual defaults, so the output keeps this style's point of view.

Design a responsive landing page for a simple task manager app in an authentic Flat Design style.

Palette: Turquoise #1ABC9C, Blue #3498DB, Amethyst #9B59B6, Sunflower #F1C40F, Carrot #E67E22, Alizarin #E74C3C, Midnight #2C3E50, Clouds #ECF0F1.
Typography: Montserrat 800 for headings, Open Sans for body.
Layout: Two-column hero: headline and intro left, a 3 by 2 grid of square flat-colour icon tiles on the right, each icon with a long diagonal shadow.
Signature details: Flat UI palette (turquoise, blue, amethyst, sunflower, carrot, alizarin on midnight and clouds), no gradients or textures, long shadows made from stacked text-shadows.
Components: Solid buttons with a thin darker bottom edge, flat cards, solid-colour alerts with white text, flat tabs with coloured underline.
Motion: Tiles flipping on the X axis in a staggered wave.
Avoid: gradients, textures, gloss, heavy drop shadows, white text on light colours.
Do not fall back on generic AI defaults: no centered hero with a gradient blob, no identical rounded cards with soft grey shadows, no purple-to-blue gradients, no emoji icons, unless this style explicitly calls for them.
Accessibility: body text contrast of at least 4.5:1, visible keyboard focus on every control, labels on all form fields, and respect prefers-reduced-motion.
Deliver one self-contained HTML file with CSS variables for every color, font, radius and shadow token.

Common mistakes

Where this style usually goes wrong, and how to keep it honest.

Invisible buttons

Flat buttons can look like labels. Give actions strong colour and clear shape.

Everything the same colour weight

Without depth, hierarchy has to come from colour and size.

Low-contrast whites

White text on yellow or turquoise can fail contrast. Check every pair.

Generic stock icons

Everyone used the same icon sets. Draw icons that fit your content.
