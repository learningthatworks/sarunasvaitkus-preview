What defines it

Art Deco mixes machine-age geometry with luxury materials. It loves symmetry, repeated lines and gold on dark, rich colours.

Sunbursts and fans

Rays spreading from a point appear on doors, posters and ceilings.

Stepped forms

Ziggurat-like steps, as in 1930s skyscraper tops, frame titles and cards.

Symmetry and repetition

Centred compositions with repeating parallel lines and chevrons.

Rich materials

Gold, black lacquer and deep emerald, like polished metal and marble.

Where it came from

Art Deco got its name decades after its heyday, from the exhibition that launched it.

  1. 1925

    The Paris exhibition

    The International Exhibition of Modern Decorative and Industrial Arts in Paris showcases the style and later gives it its name.

  2. 1930

    Towers of steel

    The Chrysler Building in New York opens with its stepped, sunburst crown.

  3. 1935

    Floating palaces

    Ocean liners like the Normandie carry Art Deco interiors across the Atlantic.

  4. 1968

    A name at last

    Historian Bevis Hillier's book popularises the term Art Deco.

  5. 2010s

    Gatsby glamour

    Films and party themes revive the look, and it returns in hotel, bar and event branding.

Motion study

Art Deco motion is ceremonial. A fan of golden rays opens like a curtain, and the title appears in the centre.

Grand Opening

Symmetry matters even in motion. Everything opens from the centre outward.

Prompt it

A ready prompt for AI design and coding tools. It names exact tokens and bans the usual defaults, so the output keeps this style's point of view.

Design a responsive landing page for a 1920s-inspired cocktail bar and jazz club in an authentic Art Deco style.

Palette: Emerald night #0E1F1B, Lacquer green #13291F, Gold #D4AF37, Antique gold #A8841F, Jade #1F6B57, Ivory #F3E9D2.
Typography: Limelight for titles in uppercase with wide tracking, Poiret One for small caps, Josefin Sans for body.
Layout: Centred hero with a slowly rotating golden sunburst behind an arched panel with a stepped crown ornament, a small tracked line, a big gold-gradient title and two buttons.
Signature details: Deep emerald background, gold gradient text, double gold frames via box-shadow, repeating vertical pinstripes, chevron and stepped ornaments.
Components: Elongated hexagon gold buttons, outlined ghost buttons, centred cards with a stepped gold ornament on top, diamond-shaped pagination, double-line alerts.
Motion: A golden fan opening from the centre, slow sunburst rotation, symmetrical reveals.
Avoid: rounded corners, pastel colours, flat grey backgrounds, feathers and champagne clip art, thin fonts for body text.
Do not fall back on generic AI defaults: no centered hero with a gradient blob, no identical rounded cards with soft grey shadows, no purple-to-blue gradients, no emoji icons, unless this style explicitly calls for them.
Accessibility: body text contrast of at least 4.5:1, visible keyboard focus on every control, labels on all form fields, and respect prefers-reduced-motion.
Deliver one self-contained HTML file with CSS variables for every color, font, radius and shadow token.

Common mistakes

Where this style usually goes wrong, and how to keep it honest.

Gold on everything

Gold loses glamour when it covers every surface. Use it for lines and highlights.

Thin fonts in paragraphs

Hairline deco fonts are for titles. Body text needs a sturdier sans.

Gatsby costume

Feathers and champagne clip art feel like a party supply shop. Let geometry carry the style.

Symmetry everywhere

Centred text over long reading areas tires the eye. Left-align longer paragraphs.
