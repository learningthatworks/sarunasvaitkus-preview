Solarpunk

A hopeful picture of the future. Buildings wear gardens, rooftops collect sunlight, wind turbines turn quietly, and technology helps nature instead of replacing it.

Open the kit Copy prompt

What defines it

Solarpunk imagines a sustainable, green future with community and craft at its heart. Visually it mixes lush plants, solar and wind tech, and soft organic curves.

Plants everywhere

Greenery grows on roofs, walls and balconies, softening every building.

Clean energy tech

Solar panels and wind turbines appear as beautiful, everyday objects.

Organic curves

Rounded towers and arches take cues from Art Nouveau rather than sharp sci-fi.

Sunny palette

Leaf greens, warm gold and clear sky blue, bright but natural.

Where it came from

Solarpunk began as an online idea and grew into a genre of stories, art and design.

  1. 1890s

    Nature in design

    Art Nouveau's love of plant forms becomes one of solarpunk's visual roots.

  2. 1970s

    Appropriate technology

    Movements for small-scale, sustainable technology shape the ideas behind the look.

  3. Late 2000s

    A name appears

    The word solarpunk starts appearing online as a hopeful answer to dark cyberpunk futures.

  4. 2014

    An aesthetic forms

    Artists share images of green cities, stained glass and solar tech, defining the visual style.

  5. 2020s

    Climate optimism

    Sustainable brands, architects and illustrators adopt the look to show positive futures.

Motion study

Solarpunk motion is renewable. A turbine spins steadily and the sun breathes, like a city running on nature's rhythm.

Continuous, calm loops suggest energy that never runs out.

Prompt it

A ready prompt for AI design and coding tools. It names exact tokens and bans the usual defaults, so the output keeps this style's point of view.

Design a responsive landing page for a community solar energy cooperative in an authentic Solarpunk style.

Palette: Morning #F6F8EE, Leaf #3E8E41, Sprout #9CCB5B, Sun gold #F4B942, Sky #BFE3F0, Solar blue #2C6E87, Forest ink #1F3B2C.
Typography: Bree Serif for headings, Sora for body.
Layout: Two-column hero on a sky-to-cream gradient: headline left, an SVG city of rounded towers covered in plants with solar panels, a glowing sun and a spinning wind turbine on the right.
Signature details: Leaf green, sprout green, sun gold and sky blue, leaf-shaped radii (large with one tight corner), soft green shadows.
Components: Leaf-shaped pill buttons, rounded cards with one tight corner, sun badges, green gradient progress bars.
Motion: A spinning turbine, a gently pulsing sun.
Avoid: neon, dark dystopian palettes, sharp chrome, greenwashing clichés, clip-art leaves.
Do not fall back on generic AI defaults: no centered hero with a gradient blob, no identical rounded cards with soft grey shadows, no purple-to-blue gradients, no emoji icons, unless this style explicitly calls for them.
Accessibility: body text contrast of at least 4.5:1, visible keyboard focus on every control, labels on all form fields, and respect prefers-reduced-motion.
Deliver one self-contained HTML file with CSS variables for every color, font, radius and shadow token.

Common mistakes

Where this style usually goes wrong, and how to keep it honest.

Greenwashing

Plants on everything can look like empty eco marketing. Show real substance.

Sci-fi coldness

Hard metal and neon break the mood. Keep technology warm and friendly.

Low contrast greens

Light greens on cream can fail contrast. Use the forest ink for text.

Clip-art leaves

Generic leaf icons feel cheap. Draw simple, consistent plant shapes.
