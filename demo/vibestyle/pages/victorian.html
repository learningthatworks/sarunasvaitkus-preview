Victorian

Ornament · Engraving · Grandeur

A style of abundance from the age of steam and print. Ornate frames, decorative type, deep jewel colors and engraved detail turn every page into a keepsake.

What defines it

Victorian design comes from the printing boom of the 19th century. It loves ornament, mixed display type and framed compositions full of detail.

Ornamental frames

Double rules, corner pieces and flourishes frame every block, like a playbill or label.

Mixed display type

Wood type, small caps and elegant serifs combine on one layout, each line a different style.

Deep jewel colors

Oxblood, forest green and navy on ivory, with gold for details.

Engraved detail

Fine lines, hatching and patterns imitate steel engraving and chromolithography.

Where it came from

The Victorian era saw printing, advertising and ornament explode together.

  1. 1837

    A long reign

    Queen Victoria takes the British throne. Her 63-year reign gives its name to an era of industry and decoration.

  2. 1851

    The Great Exhibition

    London's Crystal Palace shows off industrial goods and ornate design to millions of visitors.

  3. 1861

    Arts and Crafts

    William Morris co-founds a decorative arts firm, reacting against mass production with handmade pattern and craft.

  4. 1891

    Kelmscott Press

    Morris founds the Kelmscott Press and prints books with dense borders and medieval-inspired type.

  5. 2000s

    Steampunk and revival

    Steampunk fiction and craft brands revive Victorian ornament, often mixed with brass and machinery.

Motion study

Victorian motion is mechanical and ceremonial. Brass gears turn slowly behind an ornate frame.

Est. 1851

Use motion rarely in this style. A turning gear or a slowly revealing flourish is enough.

Common mistakes

Where this style usually goes wrong, and how to keep it honest.

Ornament over content

When frames crowd out the words, the page becomes wallpaper. Keep reading areas calm.

Too many typefaces

Real playbills mixed many faces, but screens need restraint. Three families is the limit.

Dark and small

Small serif text on dark wood fails contrast. Put reading text on ivory panels.

Costume steampunk

Random gears and goggles feel like fancy dress. Use ornament that fits the content.
