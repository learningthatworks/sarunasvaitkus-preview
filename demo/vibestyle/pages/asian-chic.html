Label print

Asian Chic

Vintage matchbox and label print from South and East Asian trade: saturated red, yellow and teal, an ornate border, and a bold short word.

Open the kit Copy the prompt
TEAEST. PORT CITY

What defines it

Asian chic here means vintage label and matchbox print: a cream ground, a hard border, and a few strong inks. The ornament is the frame, not a costume.

Print colours

Vermillion, marigold and teal at label strength, on cream. They should look like ink, not like a gradient.

Ornate borders

A double rule and small corner blocks. The frame does the decorating.

Bold display type

One short word, heavy and centered. The small print underneath is quiet.

Label logic

A stamp, a rule, a line of tiny type. That stack is the whole poster.

Where it came from

The references are trade labels and matchboxes from port cities, where colour had to read across a stall.

  1. 1800s

    Trade labels

    Tea, spice and match sellers print dense borders so a small packet can be seen.

  2. 1900s

    Letterpress ports

    Port cities mix red, yellow and green-blue inks on cheap cream paper.

  3. 1960s

    Poster words

    Display type gets heavier. A name becomes a block, not a sentence.

  4. 2010s

    Fashion looks back

    Clothing and tea brands reprint the label frame on bags and sites.

  5. Now

    Drawn frames

    The border, the bar and the stamp can be built in CSS and SVG, without a scan.

Motion study

A stamp lands: it scales down, turns a little, and settles, like a chop pressed onto paper.

TEA

One stamp. A loop of many stamps feels like a loading icon.

Prompt it

A ready prompt for Lovable and Claude Design. It names the palette, type, layout, texture, what to do and what to avoid, so the output keeps this style's point of view.

Style prompt for Lovable and Claude Design. Design a responsive landing page for a tea and spice merchant in an authentic Asian Chic style.

Palette: Cream #F6E6C4, Vermillion #D7262D, Marigold #F0B429, Teal #0E7C72, Lacquer #7A1E22, Ink #1C120C.
Typography: Dela Gothic One for the short display word, Hind for body and small print.
Layout: The hero is a label: double border, corner blocks, one red word, a marigold bar, a line of small type. Later sections keep the frame on cards.
Texture: Paper grain plus a double rule and square corner ornaments drawn in CSS. No scanned labels.
Signature details: Square corners, teal inset rule, hard offset shadow in teal, uppercase buttons.
Components: Vermillion buttons, teal secondary buttons, framed cards, square badges.
Motion: One stamp scales and rotates into place, then rests.
Do: Use the border as the ornament. Keep teal for rules and stamps. Keep red for the main word. Write real words.
Avoid: Stock dragons, random characters used as decoration, neon, thin startup sans, photos of old labels as the layout.
Do not fall back on generic AI defaults: no centered hero with a gradient blob, no identical rounded cards with soft grey shadows, no purple-to-blue gradients, no emoji icons, unless this style explicitly calls for them.
Accessibility: body text contrast of at least 4.5:1, visible keyboard focus on every control, labels on all form fields, and respect prefers-reduced-motion.
Deliver one self-contained HTML file with CSS variables for every color, font, radius and shadow token.

Common mistakes

Where this style usually goes wrong, and how to keep it honest.

Costume type

Letters from a language you are not writing are not a pattern. Use the border and the colour.

Too many inks

Red, yellow and teal are enough. A fourth bright hue turns the label into a poster mess.

Thin modern type

A light geometric sans loses the print. The display word has to be heavy.

Scanned antiques

A photo of a real matchbox is someone else's work. Draw the frame yourself.
