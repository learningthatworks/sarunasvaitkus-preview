Foil and pearl

Holographic Iridescence

A pearl ground and one surface that shifts colour, the way printed foil does when you tilt it.

See the foil Copy the prompt
IRIStilt to read the colour

What defines it

Holographic iridescence is printed foil brought to the screen. The page stays pearl and quiet. One surface carries the rainbow.

Foil shift

Colour moves as if the card tilted. Cyan, violet, rose and gold pass through one highlight.

Pearl ground

Off-white paper lets the sheen show. A black page turns this into a different style.

Thin type

Light weight and wide tracking. The letters should feel stamped, not shouted.

One sheen

A single moving highlight. Confetti and extra rainbows weaken it.

Where it came from

The effect comes from real foil and diffraction print, then from packaging, then from screens that can fake a tilt.

  1. 1960s

    Diffraction foil

    Printers lay thin films on paper that split light into colour.

  2. 1990s

    Stickers

    Holographic stickers spread on toys, tickets and collectibles.

  3. 2016

    Beauty packaging

    Fragrance and makeup boxes use foil panels on pale cartons.

  4. 2020

    Screen foil

    Designers rebuild the tilt with conic gradients and a moving white band.

  5. 2023

    One panel

    Product sites keep the rest of the page quiet and spend the effect on a single card.

Motion study

A white band crosses the foil, and the hue drifts a little. It should feel like a slow tilt, not a loop you notice.

If the sweep is faster than a breath, it starts to flash. Slow it down.

Prompt it

A ready prompt for Lovable and Claude Design. It names the palette, type, layout, texture, what to do and what to avoid, so the output keeps this style's point of view.

Style prompt for Lovable and Claude Design. Design a responsive landing page for a small fragrance studio in an authentic Holographic Iridescence style.

Palette: Pearl #F6F3EE, Ink #1A1520, Cyan foil #7AD7FF, Violet foil #C9A4FF, Rose foil #FFB3D4, Gold foil #FFE08A.
Typography: Jost light, wide-tracked capitals for the name. Outfit for body text.
Layout: Split hero on pearl. The right side is one foil card with a thin word and a small caption. Later sections are calm columns.
Texture: A conic foil gradient with a soft white band sweeping across it. Fine, not glitter.
Signature details: Soft card radius, a sheen overlay that does not block clicks, foil only in media and the hero card.
Components: Ink buttons with wide tracking, pale cards, small pills. Rainbow is not used for button fills.
Motion: A seven-second sheen. Hue drifts slightly. No spinning wheels.
Do: Keep reading text in ink. Spend the rainbow on one surface. Let the highlight move slowly.
Avoid: Rainbow paragraph text, glitter photographs, glassmorphism blur, chrome Y2K bubbles, fast hue spins.
Do not fall back on generic AI defaults: no centered hero with a gradient blob, no identical rounded cards with soft grey shadows, no purple-to-blue gradients, no emoji icons, unless this style explicitly calls for them.
Accessibility: body text contrast of at least 4.5:1, visible keyboard focus on every control, labels on all form fields, and respect prefers-reduced-motion.
Deliver one self-contained HTML file with CSS variables for every color, font, radius and shadow token.

Common mistakes

Where this style usually goes wrong, and how to keep it honest.

Rainbow text

Foil colours fail as small type. The sheen belongs on a shape behind the words.

Glitter photos

A photo of sparkle is a different texture. Draw the foil in gradients.

Everywhere

If every card is foil, the eye has nowhere to rest. Use one panel.

Too fast

A quick loop feels like a warning light. The tilt should be slow.
