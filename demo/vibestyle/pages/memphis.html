Memphis
Design

Loud, silly and proud of it. Memphis mixes squiggles, confetti, terrazzo speckles and clashing pastels into objects and pages that refuse to be serious.

Open the kit Copy prompt

What defines it

Memphis Design began as a furniture collective in Milan that rejected good taste. Its shapes and patterns became a graphic language of fun.

Squiggles and zigzags

Hand-drawn wavy lines and sharp zigzags scatter across surfaces as pure decoration.

Terrazzo and confetti

Speckled patterns of colourful chips imitate cheap laminate and stone.

Clashing pastels

Pink, teal, yellow and cobalt sit side by side with black outlines.

Toy-like geometry

Cylinders, cones and spheres combine like building blocks.

Where it came from

Memphis lasted only a few years as a group, but its look keeps coming back.

  1. 1980

    A name from a song

    Designers meet at Ettore Sottsass's flat in Milan, and a Bob Dylan song about the Memphis blues gives the group its name.

  2. 1981

    First show

    Memphis presents its furniture in Milan, including bookcases that look more like totems than shelves.

  3. 1988

    Group disbands

    The collective breaks up after a short, famous run.

  4. 1990s

    TV and packaging

    The squiggles and pastels spread into television graphics, school supplies and fashion.

  5. 2010s

    Revival

    Brands and illustrators revive the shapes for social media graphics and websites.

Motion study

Memphis motion bops. Shapes bounce and tilt with an exaggerated overshoot, like toys on a spring.

The overshoot comes from cubic-bezier(.3, 1.8, .5, 1).

Prompt it

A ready prompt for AI design and coding tools. It names exact tokens and bans the usual defaults, so the output keeps this style's point of view.

Design a responsive landing page for a playful design shop for kids' furniture in an authentic Memphis Design style.

Palette: Pink #FF9EC7, Teal #3CC6C0, Yellow #FFD23F, Cobalt #2D5BFF, Black #111111, White #FFFFFF.
Typography: Dela Gothic One for headlines, Lexend for body.
Layout: Two-column hero: stacked headline with one word on a tilted yellow sticker, an outlined text panel with a teal offset shadow, and a scatter of Memphis shapes on the right (a black squiggle, a terrazzo circle, a striped triangle, a teal cylinder, a zigzag).
Signature details: Pink #FF9EC7, teal #3CC6C0, yellow #FFD23F, cobalt #2D5BFF with 3px black outlines, coloured offset shadows, confetti dots on a white page.
Components: Outlined buttons with black offset shadows that tilt on hover, cards with rotating pink, teal and yellow shadows, round tilted badges, striped progress bars.
Motion: Bouncy overshoot on a few shapes, a slowly spinning striped triangle.
Avoid: text on patterns, gradients (except cylinder shading), thin lines, beige, copies of famous furniture.
Do not fall back on generic AI defaults: no centered hero with a gradient blob, no identical rounded cards with soft grey shadows, no purple-to-blue gradients, no emoji icons, unless this style explicitly calls for them.
Accessibility: body text contrast of at least 4.5:1, visible keyboard focus on every control, labels on all form fields, and respect prefers-reduced-motion.
Deliver one self-contained HTML file with CSS variables for every color, font, radius and shadow token.

Common mistakes

Where this style usually goes wrong, and how to keep it honest.

Confetti behind text

Patterns behind paragraphs make reading hard. Put text on solid panels.

Everything bouncing

Motion on every shape feels chaotic. Animate a few hero shapes only.

Only decoration

Memphis still needs clear hierarchy. Keep one main action obvious.
