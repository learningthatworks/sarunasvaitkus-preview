Paper Cut

Scenes built from layers of cut paper. Each layer casts a soft shadow on the one behind it, so flat shapes gain depth, like a pop-up book or a shadow box.

Open the kit Copy the prompt

What defines it

Paper cut design stacks flat shapes into layers. Soft shadows between them make a simple colour palette feel handmade and three-dimensional.

Stacked layers

Shapes sit on top of each other, from light in the back to dark at the front.

Soft layer shadows

Each layer casts a short, soft shadow on the one below.

Organic cut edges

Curves look cut with scissors or a craft knife, never perfectly geometric.

Tonal palette

Several shades of one or two colours create depth, with one warm accent.

Where it came from

Paper cutting is an ancient craft that designers rediscovered once screens could render soft shadows cheaply.

  1. 500s+

    Folk craft

    Paper cutting grows into a folk art in China and later spreads to many cultures.

  2. 1926

    Silhouette film

    Lotte Reiniger releases The Adventures of Prince Achmed, animated entirely with cut-out silhouettes.

  3. 1940s

    Cut-outs in art

    Henri Matisse turns to painted paper cut-outs late in his career.

  4. 2010s

    Craft illustration

    Illustrators use real and digital paper layers for editorial art and advertising.

  5. 2020s

    CSS layers

    drop-shadow filters and stacked SVG paths make paper-cut scenes easy to build on the web.

Motion study

Paper cut motion works like a pop-up book. Layers rise from the bottom one after another and settle into place.

Each layer starts 0.25 seconds after the previous one, building depth from back to front.

Components: Pill buttons that press down, cards with a second paper sheet behind, inset text fields, soft alerts with pill labels.
Motion: Layers rising one by one like a pop-up book, 0.25 second stagger.
Avoid: hard black outlines, gradients inside shapes, neon colours, too many shadow layers.
Do not fall back on generic AI defaults: no centered hero with a gradient blob, no identical rounded cards with soft grey shadows, no purple-to-blue gradients, no emoji icons, unless this style explicitly calls for them.
Accessibility: body text contrast of at least 4.5:1, visible keyboard focus on every control, labels on all form fields, and respect prefers-reduced-motion.

Common mistakes

Where this style usually goes wrong, and how to keep it honest.

Shadow mud

Too many dark shadows make a scene muddy. Keep them short and light.

Too many layers

Beyond five or six layers the scene looks cluttered. Simplify.

Low contrast text

Text on mid-tone paper can fail contrast. Put it on the lightest layer.

Heavy filters

Many drop-shadow filters slow down phones. Keep large animated scenes small.
