Low-poly

Scenes built from flat triangles. Early 3D graphics could only afford a few polygons, and designers turned that limit into a crisp, faceted style.

Open the kit Copy prompt

What defines it

Low-poly art builds shapes from a small number of flat-shaded triangles. Each facet gets one colour, and light is shown by changing the shade from facet to facet.

Flat facets

Every triangle has a single solid colour with no smooth gradient inside it.

Shade as light

A facet facing the light is paler, one facing away is darker. That alone creates volume.

Few, large shapes

Detail is reduced on purpose. A mountain might be a few dozen triangles.

Angular edges

Buttons and cards borrow the cut corners and diagonals of the triangles.

Where it came from

Low-poly started as a technical limit of early 3D and returned as a deliberate art style.

  1. 1934

    Triangulation

    Mathematician Boris Delaunay describes the triangulation method still used to turn points into neat triangle meshes.

  2. 1990s

    Early 3D games

    Home consoles render characters and worlds with a few hundred polygons, creating the classic faceted look.

  3. 2010s

    Indie revival

    Indie games and illustrators choose low-poly for its clarity and small file sizes.

  4. 2020s

    Web 3D

    WebGL and SVG make faceted backgrounds and hero scenes easy to generate in the browser.

Motion study

Low-poly motion is shimmer. Facets brighten and dim at different times, like light moving over a crystal.

Each triangle runs the same brightness animation with a different delay.

Prompt it

A ready prompt for AI design and coding tools. It names exact tokens and bans the usual defaults, so the output keeps this style's point of view.

Design a responsive landing page for an outdoor adventure and hiking app in an authentic Low-poly style.

Palette: Night blue #1B2A49, Deep blue #2C4470, Blue #3E5C8A, Sky #7FA3C8, Peach #F2B880, Coral #E07A5F, Mist #EEF1F6.
Typography: Saira 800 for headings, Nunito Sans for body.
Layout: Full-bleed low-poly mountain landscape as the hero background, generated as SVG triangles shaded by height, with a solid angular card on the left holding the headline and buttons.
Signature details: Flat-shaded triangles in a blue ramp with peach and coral highlights, cut-corner cards and badges via clip-path, soft shadows.
Components: Parallelogram buttons with a two-tone diagonal split, faceted cards, diamond pagination, triangular switch thumbs.
Motion: Triangles shimmering with staggered brightness changes.
Avoid: smooth gradients inside shapes, rounded corners, tiny noisy facets, text on the mesh.
Do not fall back on generic AI defaults: no centered hero with a gradient blob, no identical rounded cards with soft grey shadows, no purple-to-blue gradients, no emoji icons, unless this style explicitly calls for them.
Accessibility: body text contrast of at least 4.5:1, visible keyboard focus on every control, labels on all form fields, and respect prefers-reduced-motion.
Deliver one self-contained HTML file with CSS variables for every color, font, radius and shadow token.

Common mistakes

Where this style usually goes wrong, and how to keep it honest.

Too many triangles

Hundreds of tiny facets look noisy. Keep triangles large.

Gradients inside facets

Smooth gradients break the look. One flat colour per triangle.
