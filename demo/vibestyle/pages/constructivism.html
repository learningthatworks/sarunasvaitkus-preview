Constructivism

Posters built like machines. Red and black blocks, sharp diagonals, bold type and photomontage turned 1920s graphic design into pure energy and movement.

Open the kit Copy prompt

What defines it

Constructivism treated design as construction. Artists assembled posters from basic shapes, diagonal type and photos to create maximum visual force.

Dynamic diagonals

Type and shapes run at steep angles, so the page feels like it is moving.

Red, black and paper

A tight palette of red and black on off-white newsprint.

Bold geometric type

Heavy, blocky letters, often rotated or stacked.

Wedges and circles

Sharp triangles pierce circles, a signature graphic device.

Where it came from

Constructivism grew out of art experiments in Russia around 1915 and shaped modern graphic design worldwide.

  1. 1915

    Art as construction

    Artists start building abstract works from industrial materials instead of painting pictures.

  2. 1919

    The red wedge

    El Lissitzky's poster of a red wedge piercing a white circle becomes a landmark of abstract graphic design.

  3. 1920s

    Photomontage and ads

    Alexander Rodchenko combines photos, diagonals and bold type in posters and advertisements.

  4. 1930s

    Pushed aside

    Official art policy favours realistic painting, and the movement fades at home.

  5. 2000s

    Graphic revival

    Album covers, posters and fashion borrow the diagonals and red-black palette.

Motion study

Constructivist motion is force. A red wedge drives into a black circle and the circle shudders on impact.

Hard, mechanical easing makes the impact feel physical.

Prompt it

A ready prompt for AI design and coding tools. It names exact tokens and bans the usual defaults, so the output keeps this style's point of view.

Design a responsive landing page for an independent film festival in an authentic Constructivism style.

Palette: Paper #ECE4D0, Red #D0271D, Black #111111, Newsprint #E0D6BE, Grey #3F3A33.
Typography: Russo One uppercase for headings, IBM Plex Sans Condensed for body.
Layout: Hero with a red wedge thrusting in from the left, a big black halftone disc on the right, a tilted black bar across the bottom and a rotated two-colour headline over a boxed intro.
Signature details: Red #D0271D and black on newsprint paper, steep diagonals, 3px black borders, halftone dot fills.
Components: Skewed uppercase buttons, cards with thick red top bars, rotated red badges, diagonal striped progress bars.
Motion: A red wedge driving into a circle with hard mechanical easing.
Avoid: political symbols and slogans, rounded corners, pastel colours, rotated paragraphs.
Do not fall back on generic AI defaults: no centered hero with a gradient blob, no identical rounded cards with soft grey shadows, no purple-to-blue gradients, no emoji icons, unless this style explicitly calls for them.
Accessibility: body text contrast of at least 4.5:1, visible keyboard focus on every control, labels on all form fields, and respect prefers-reduced-motion.
Deliver one self-contained HTML file with CSS variables for every color, font, radius and shadow token.

Common mistakes

Where this style usually goes wrong, and how to keep it honest.

Political symbols

The movement is tied to politics. Avoid state symbols and slogans unless that is your topic.

Rotated body text

Tilt headings and blocks. Keep paragraphs level for reading.

Too much red

Red works as a strike. Large red areas tire the eye.

Photo copyright

Historic photomontages use protected photos. Make your own.
