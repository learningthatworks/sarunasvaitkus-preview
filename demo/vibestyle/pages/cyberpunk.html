Cyberpunk

High tech, low life. Cyberpunk interfaces are dense, angular and lit by neon signs in the rain, with a sense that the system is powerful and not entirely on your side.

Jack in Copy prompt

What defines it

Cyberpunk design comes from science fiction about megacities and hackers. It pairs aggressive angles and warning colors with heads-up display details.

Clipped angles

Corners are cut at 45 degrees on buttons, cards and frames, like military hardware.

Warning palette

Hazard yellow, cyan and red on near-black. Colors signal status, danger and access.

HUD detail

Monospace data readouts, scan lines, brackets and coordinates layer information over everything.

Urban density

Neon signage, multilingual text and rain-soaked cityscapes create a crowded, nocturnal world.

Where it came from

Cyberpunk began in fiction and film and became a visual language through games.

  1. 1980

    A word is born

    Bruce Bethke writes a short story called Cyberpunk, published in 1983, which gives the genre its name.

  2. 1982

    Blade Runner

    Ridley Scott's film shows a rainy, neon-lit, overcrowded future city that defines the look for decades.

  3. 1984

    Neuromancer

    William Gibson's novel popularises cyberspace and the hacker hero.

  4. 1988

    Akira

    Katsuhiro Otomo's animated film brings cyberpunk's motorcycles, megacities and neon to global audiences.

  5. 2020

    Cyberpunk 2077

    The video game turns the aesthetic into a complete interface language of angled panels and yellow warnings.

Motion study

Cyberpunk motion is scanning and glitching. Lines sweep across displays, counters blink and titles tear for a split second.

SCAN // SECTOR 798%

Glitches last under 200ms and happen rarely. Constant flicker is exhausting.

Common mistakes

Where this style usually goes wrong, and how to keep it honest.

Aggressive by default

Hazard yellow everywhere turns every message into a warning. Reserve it for primary actions.

Fake languages

Random Asian characters as decoration can be meaningless or offensive. Use text you understand.

Unreadable condensed type

Tight uppercase text at small sizes is hard to read. Keep body text in sentence case.

Too much glitch

Glitches need rhythm and restraint, or they read as bugs.
